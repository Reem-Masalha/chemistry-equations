01 · COMBUSTION

Balance C and H before O

For hydrocarbons reacting with oxygen, carbon is usually easiest to balance first, then hydrogen, and oxygen last. If oxygen becomes odd, a temporary fraction can simplify the calculation.

C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Balance carbon: 3CO₂. Balance hydrogen: 4H₂O. The products contain 10 oxygen atoms, so use 5O₂.

C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O

Practice

  1. Balance CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.
  2. Balance C₂H₅OH + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.
  3. Explain why oxygen is usually left until last.
02 · REDOX

Track atoms and electron transfer

Redox reactions involve oxidation and reduction. In addition to conserving atoms, ionic equations must conserve charge. Oxidation states can help identify which species are oxidized and reduced.

Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu

Zinc loses two electrons and copper ions gain two. Atoms and total charge are both conserved.

Practice

  1. Identify the species oxidized in Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu.
  2. Identify the species reduced.
  3. Why must charge be checked in an ionic equation?
03 · COMPLEX IONIC EQUATIONS

Balance atoms and charge

For ionic equations, conserving atoms is not enough: the total charge on both sides must also match. In acidic solution, H₂O and H⁺ can be used to balance oxygen and hydrogen; electrons are used to balance charge in half-reaction methods.

MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ + H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺ + H₂O

Use half-reactions systematically rather than guessing coefficients. Balance elements other than H and O first, then O with water, H with H⁺, and charge with electrons.

Practice

  1. Why must charge be conserved?
  2. What is used to balance oxygen in acidic solution?
  3. What is used to balance charge in the half-reaction method?
04 · SYSTEMATIC BALANCING

Use algebra when guessing becomes difficult

Assign an unknown coefficient to every formula, write one conservation equation for each element, and solve the resulting system. Finally, scale the solution to the smallest positive whole-number coefficients.

aFe + bO₂ → cFe₂O₃

Iron gives a = 2c. Oxygen gives 2b = 3c. Choosing c = 2 produces a = 4 and b = 3.

4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃

Practice

  1. Set up conservation equations for C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.
  2. Why must the final coefficients be positive?
  3. Why should the final ratio be reduced to the smallest whole numbers?
