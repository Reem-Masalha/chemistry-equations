01 · POLYATOMIC IONS

Recognize groups that can stay together

Polyatomic ions are groups of atoms that carry an overall charge, such as SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻ and OH⁻. In many equations, the same polyatomic ion appears unchanged on both sides. When that happens, treating the group as one unit can make balancing easier.

NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O

OH and H₂O must still be counted atom by atom when needed. Do not change a subscript inside a chemical formula.

Practice

  1. How many oxygen atoms are in 2SO₄?
  2. What atoms are in NO₃?
  3. Why can an unchanged polyatomic group sometimes be balanced as a unit?
02 · PARENTHESES

Count everything inside a group

A subscript outside parentheses multiplies every atom inside the parentheses.

Ca(OH)₂

Ca(OH)₂ contains 1 Ca, 2 O and 2 H. The 2 applies to both O and H.

Al₂(SO₄)₃

Al₂(SO₄)₃ contains 2 Al, 3 S and 12 O.

Practice

  1. Count every atom in Mg(OH)₂.
  2. Count every atom in Al₂(SO₄)₃.
  3. Why does the 3 outside SO₄ multiply oxygen as well as sulfur?
03 · FRACTIONAL COEFFICIENTS

Use fractions when they simplify the process

Sometimes a fractional coefficient is convenient during balancing. Fractions are temporary: after the equation is balanced, multiply every coefficient by the denominator needed to produce the smallest whole-number ratio.

C₂H₆ + 7/2 O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O

Multiplying every coefficient by 2 gives:

2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O

Practice

  1. Why can a fraction be useful while balancing?
  2. What must you do before giving the final answer?
  3. Turn 1/2, 3 and 2 into the smallest whole-number ratio.
04 · COMPLICATED REACTIONS

Choose an order and check repeatedly

For larger equations, start with an element that appears in fewer formulas. Leave hydrogen and oxygen until later when they occur in many compounds. After each coefficient change, recount every affected element.

Fe₂(SO₄)₃ + KOH → Fe(OH)₃ + K₂SO₄

A useful strategy is to recognize SO₄ as an unchanged group, balance Fe, then OH, then K, and finally verify every element.

Practice

  1. Which element or group would you balance first and why?
  2. Balance Fe₂(SO₄)₃ + KOH → Fe(OH)₃ + K₂SO₄.
  3. How can you verify your final coefficients?
