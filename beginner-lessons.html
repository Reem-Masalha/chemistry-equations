A detailed beginner path for understanding chemical equations and learning how to balance them.

01 · WHAT IS A CHEMICAL EQUATION?

Describe what happens in a reaction

A chemical equation is a short way of showing a chemical reaction. The substances you start with are called reactants, and the substances formed are called products. An arrow shows the direction from reactants to products.

H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

The equation tells us which substances participate in the reaction, but it is not balanced yet. A balanced equation must contain the same number of atoms of every element on both sides.

How to read it

Read from left to right: reactants → products. The plus sign separates substances on the same side, while the arrow separates reactants from products.

Practice

  1. What does the arrow in a chemical equation show?
  2. What are the substances on the left called?
  3. What are the substances on the right called?
02 · READING CHEMICAL FORMULAS

Understand subscripts

A chemical formula tells you which elements are present and how many atoms of each element are in one particle of the substance. A small number written after an element is a subscript.

H₂O

H₂O contains 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom. When there is no subscript, the number is 1.

In Ca(OH)₂, the subscript 2 applies to the entire OH group, so the formula contains 1 Ca, 2 O and 2 H.

Coefficient vs. subscript

A coefficient is placed in front of a formula and multiplies the whole formula. A subscript is part of the formula. Never change a subscript while balancing because that changes the substance.

Practice

  1. How many oxygen atoms are in Ca(OH)₂?
  2. How many hydrogen atoms are in 3H₂O?
  3. What does the coefficient in 3H₂O multiply?
  4. Why should a subscript not be changed while balancing?
03 · CONSERVATION OF MASS

Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed

In an ordinary chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged into new combinations. Therefore, every element must have the same total number of atoms before and after the reaction.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Count hydrogen: the left side has 4 H atoms and the right side has 4 H atoms. Count oxygen: the left side has 2 O atoms and the right side has 2 O atoms. The equation is balanced.

Why coefficients work

A coefficient tells you how many particles or formula units are present. It multiplies every atom in the formula without changing the substance itself.

Practice

  1. Why must each element have the same atom count on both sides?
  2. How many H atoms are in 2H₂?
  3. How many O atoms are in 2H₂O?
  4. Is H₂ + O₂ → H₂O balanced? Explain by counting atoms.
04 · HOW TO BALANCE A CHEMICAL EQUATION

Use a reliable step-by-step method

Balancing is not about changing the substances. It is about finding coefficients that make the number of atoms of every element equal on both sides.

Step 1 — Write the correct formulas

Make sure every element symbol and chemical formula is correct before balancing. Remember that capitalization matters: Co is cobalt, while CO represents carbon monoxide.

Step 2 — Count every atom

Write down the number of atoms of each element on the reactant side and product side.

Step 3 — Add coefficients

Place whole-number coefficients in front of formulas. Do not change subscripts.

Step 4 — Recount all elements

After every change, count the atoms again.

Step 5 — Check the final equation

Make sure every element has exactly the same number of atoms on both sides and that the coefficients are in the smallest whole-number ratio.

Worked example

H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

1. Put 2 before H₂O.

H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

2. Put 2 before H₂.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

3. H = 4 on both sides and O = 2 on both sides.

Practice

  1. Balance Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃.
  2. Balance H₂ + Cl₂ → HCl.
  3. Balance N₂ + H₂ → NH₃.
05 · DIATOMIC ELEMENTS

Know the elements that occur as pairs

Hydrogen H₂, nitrogen N₂, oxygen O₂, fluorine F₂, chlorine Cl₂, bromine Br₂ and iodine I₂ normally occur as diatomic molecules when they are free elements.

H₂ · N₂ · O₂ · F₂ · Cl₂ · Br₂ · I₂

Practice

  1. Which formula represents elemental oxygen?
  2. Which formula represents elemental chlorine?
  3. Balance H₂ + O₂ → H₂O.
  4. Balance N₂ + H₂ → NH₃.
06 · PUTTING IT ALL TOGETHER

Your complete balancing checklist

Before balancing, check the formulas and element symbols. Then count atoms, use coefficients, and check the final equation.

Balancing checklist

  • ✓ Correct element symbols
  • ✓ Correct chemical formulas and subscripts
  • ✓ Correct diatomic formulas when applicable
  • ✓ Count atoms on both sides
  • ✓ Add coefficients instead of changing formulas
  • ✓ Recount every element
  • ✓ Use the smallest whole-number ratio
